# Phase 5: bullet construction

## The rules

1. **One win per bullet.** Four to six per role, the biggest things done there, never a cram of everything.
2. **One doubt each.** Name the doubt before writing the bullet. If none, cut it.
3. **Verb first, always.** A plain past-tense verb opens every bullet. No "I", "me" or "my" anywhere, no "Responsible for", and never the number as the first word.
4. **Measured result:** result verb, the number, then "by" and how. "Grew weekly active users 5x (20 to 100) by..." The win lands in the first half of the first line because the number sits right after the verb.
5. **No measured result:** "Built [thing] that [does a, b and c]." List what it does. Never imply an outcome nobody measured.
6. **Keep the detail after "by".** Two printed lines is fine when the how is what creates belief.
7. **One sentence.** Self-contained, no bullet depends on another to make sense.
8. **Order by the rule in SKILL.md.**

## House style

Calibrated by A/B testing a real candidate. These are the defaults; when the candidate has bullets of their own, measure their pattern first and match it.

| Rule | Write | Not |
|---|---|---|
| Plain verbs | Built, Grew, Ran, Shipped, Cut, Trained, Mapped, Connected, Added | Architected, Engineered, Leveraged, Spearheaded, Delivered, Exposed, Enabled |
| Repeated "Built" is fine | Accuracy beats verb variety | Swapping in a fancier synonym to avoid a repeat |
| Name the tech | "through MCP and read-only SQL over client-scoped warehouses" | "with secure read-only access" |
| Competitor numbers | "found Acme cited in 6 of 40 answers (vs. Globex 16 and Initech 13)" | A colon, a second sentence, or unnamed rivals |
| Numbers | Digits and "~": "~1,000", "3 months" | "roughly one thousand", "three months" |
| Second accomplishment | Join with ", and" | A semicolon or a second sentence |
| Stopping a project | "Ran X..., then killed it after data showed..." | Opening with "Killed", or "shut it down", "sunset" |
| Ratios | "5x (20 to 100)", the base in parentheses | A ratio with no base |
| Ending | Every bullet ends with a period | |

## What reads as AI-written

None of these go in:

- **Number-first openers:** "6 of 40 buyer questions named..." instead of a verb.
- **Labels:** "Result: ...", "Impact: ...".
- **"X, not Y" contrasts:** "distribution, not autonomy".
- **Three-beat lists** used for rhythm, not because there are three things.
- **Dashes, slashes and made-up compounds:** em dashes, "company/startup/agency", "source-traceable". Write "and" or "or".
- **A colon joining a second thought.** A colon only before a list in parentheses.
- **A different author's shape.** If an edited bullet does not read like the candidate's untouched ones, it is wrong even when the facts are right.

## Calibrating to the candidate

When the candidate's taste is unclear, A/B test before rewriting. One variable per round, using their real bullets with the facts unchanged: opening verb, how much detail, wording for no result, wording for stopping a project, how much tech to name, comparisons, verb register, number format, joining clauses. Then play back the locked rules and get a yes before applying them. Do the same for ordering, one role at a time. Never invent a fact for a test option, and say so if one slipped in.

## Thin roles

When a role has fewer real wins than there are doubts, write fewer bullets. Never pad a role. A role whose every bullet is Weak becomes a header-only line, or gets cut, and its space goes to a role that can carry proof.

## Keywords

Each bullet carries the words the committee uses for the proof that kills its doubt, taken from the committee map and not from the candidate's old resume. A keyword goes in only if it is true. Untrue ones become questions for the candidate or things to go build, listed in the audit.

## Attribution

Say what the candidate owned versus what the team, the market or a tailwind did. Honest attribution is itself proof against "no-name company, inflated numbers". Where a market tailwind or headwind exists, name it in the bullet ("while the category shrank 20%") or have the answer ready.

## Never

- Cite test counts, node counts, contacts enriched, emails sent or any other volume number as proof. Volume without an outcome reads as spam, and reviewers know volume can be inflated.
- Count test cases or demos as users.
- Write euphoria that maps to no doubt.
- Invent a number, a user or a technique to create the wow. Ask instead.
- Claim customers, adoption or revenue that does not exist.

## Length-neutral editing

Once a page is full, every rewrite has to hold its line count. Check the rendered page, not the source text, and watch for a last word left alone on its own line. Cut or reword the same bullet to fix it, not a neighbouring one.

## Filling the page

When the page has room left, fill it by changing sizes, never words. Search text size, line spacing, heading sizes, section gaps and side margins together, and keep the combination that fills the most without spilling to page 2 or leaving a single word alone on a line. Narrower side margins usually buy the larger text size. Leave about 2 lines of slack, since live previews paginate slightly differently from a print.

Make each round of changes in a new version and leave the previous one untouched.
