# Phase 5: bullet construction

## The rules

1. **One win per bullet.** Four or five per role, the biggest things done there, never a cram of everything.
2. **Every bullet is a Pain, a Problem, a Solution or a Result**, and should make the reader think "wow".
3. **Open on the highest available rung:** Result, then Solution, then Problem, then Pain. Drop a rung only when the one above is not honestly available.
4. **The win lands in the first half of the first line.** Readers skim in an F pattern and read the first words of each line and the first bullet of each role. Lead with the number, the pain or the clever idea, then give the how.
5. **Start with an active verb matched to the type**, varied across the page.
6. **Self-contained.** No bullet depends on another to make sense.
7. **One doubt each.** Name the doubt before writing the bullet. If none, cut it.
8. **Order by obviousness**, per the rule in SKILL.md.
9. **Prefer one line.** Two only when the extra detail is what creates belief.

## Thin roles

When a role has fewer real wins than there are doubts, write fewer bullets. Never pad a role to reach four. A role whose every bullet is Weak becomes a header-only line, or gets cut, and its space goes to a role that can carry proof.

## Verb bank

| Type | Verbs |
|---|---|
| Result | Grew, Drove, Generated, Scaled, Secured, Delivered |
| Solution | Engineered, Designed, Architected, Shipped, Built, Produced, Instrumented |
| Problem | Tackled, Exposed, Untangled, Stood up, Diagnosed, Traced |
| Pain | Rescued, Broke, Ended, Eliminated, Killed, Blocked |

Never reuse a verb on the same page.

## Keywords

Each bullet carries the words the committee uses for the proof that kills its doubt, taken from the committee map and not from the candidate's old resume. A keyword goes in only if it is true. Untrue ones become questions for the candidate or things to go build, listed in the audit.

## Attribution

Say what the candidate owned versus what the team, the market or a tailwind did. Honest attribution is itself proof against "no-name company, inflated numbers". Where a market tailwind exists, expect the reader to ask about it, and have the answer ready.

## Never

- Cite test counts, node counts, contacts enriched, emails sent or any other volume number as proof. Volume without an outcome reads as spam, and reviewers know volume can be inflated.
- Write euphoria that maps to no doubt.
- Invent a number, a user or a technique to create the wow. Ask instead.
- Claim customers, adoption or revenue that does not exist.

## Length-neutral editing

Once a page is full, every rewrite has to hold its line count. Check the rendered page, not the source text, and watch for a last word left alone on its own line. Cut or reword the same bullet to fix it, not a neighbouring one.
