# The keyword sheet

Every doubt chain carries two keyword sets, and both go on the page:

- **Doubt keywords** - the words the reader screens and red-flags on. These decide whether the resume gets read at all, and they belong in the bullet that kills that doubt.
- **Proof keywords** - the words that describe the wow. These decide whether the reader gets excited.

A bullet with proof keywords but no doubt keywords never gets found. A bullet with doubt keywords but no proof keywords gets found and then forgotten.

## Three sources, cross-checked

Build one sheet per application, merging three sources. A term's weight comes from how many of them it appears in.

| Source | What it gives | How to get it |
|---|---|---|
| The committee map | What the field wants, untainted by the candidate's own resume | Phase 1 research, counted across postings |
| The specific job description | The exact words this employer used, including their internal names for things | Read the posting in full. Count repeats: what they say twice, they care about |
| Search and screening terms | What a recruiter types to find people, and what an applicant tracking system matches on | Recruiter boolean searches, the posting's "skills" tags, titles of adjacent postings, what practitioners call the work |

## The sheet

| Term | In the map | In this JD | Searched on | True for the candidate | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Rules:

1. **Three sources beats one.** A term in all three goes in first, wherever it is true.
2. **Only if true.** An untrue term is a gap, not a word choice. It becomes a question or something to go build, listed in the audit.
3. **JD-only terms are real but narrow.** Use the employer's exact word when it is true, since a human reads for their own vocabulary and a machine matches strings.
4. **Map-only terms still matter.** They are what the field wants even when this posting forgot to say it, and they carry over to the next application.
5. **Never pad.** Keyword stuffing reads as noise and fails the one-win rule. Each term rides inside a bullet that is already killing a doubt.

## Cross-check, both directions

- Terms in the JD that appear nowhere on the resume: either add them where true, or list them as gaps.
- Terms on the resume that appear in no source: cut or replace with the reader's word for the same thing.
- Terms the candidate uses that the field calls something else: rename to the field's word.

The audit reports both lists. Missing keywords show in the bullet matrix; the full sheet ships alongside it.
