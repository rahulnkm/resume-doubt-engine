# Phase 6: the audit

Five outputs, in this order.

## 1. Bullet matrix

One row per bullet, in page order.

| Bullet | Type | Hook rung | Doubt it kills (persona) | Obviousness | Proof level | Missing keywords |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Proof level is one of:

- **Euphoria** - reaches what makes that reader say wow
- **Passes** - gets past the doubt without the wow
- **Weak** - gestures at the doubt, no proof behind it
- **Hurts** - triggers a doubt instead of killing one, usually volume with no outcome
- **None** - maps to no doubt in the map; reframe or cut

## 2. Coverage

One row per doubt in the map, including the ones nothing answers.

| Doubt | Status | Covered by |
|---|---|---|

Status: Strong, Partial, Weak, None, or Over-covered. Then state the pattern in the empty ones, since several holes usually share a cause, and check each role against the ordering rule.

## 3. Quick fixes

Changes available today with the facts already in hand. Each one names the doubt it serves. If the page is full, they hold the line count. Any suggested bullet text is already written in the house style from `bullet-style.md`, so it reads like the candidate wrote it.

## 4. Fact-check landmines

Everything a reader could check and find shaky: unverifiable numbers, single-run measurements, claims whose wording implies more than the facts support, dates that disagree with the candidate's public profiles, gaps and short tenures. For each, either fix the wording or give the candidate the one-line answer to have ready.

## 5. Story prompts

Ranked by how many doubts each would fill. Each prompt is a concrete question the candidate can answer from memory, plus the doubts it would kill. Keep asking about outcomes rather than activities: who used it, what changed, what broke, what it earned.

Separate out anything that is not a story but an action, such as getting a first user, or asking a former colleague to vouch. Those create proof that no rewrite can.
